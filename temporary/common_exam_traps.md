# Common Exam Traps in Tunisian Baccalaureate

Based on an analysis of past exams and preparatory materials, students frequently fall into the following traps:

## Mathematics
1. **Domain of Definition:** Forgetting to define the domain of a function before analyzing limits or derivatives, especially with `ln(x)` and `sqrt(x)`.
2. **Complex Numbers:**
   - Mixing up the algebraic form and the exponential form during calculations.
   - Forgetting that the argument is defined modulo 2π, which affects geometric interpretations (angles).
3. **Integration:** Missing the constant of integration `C` when calculating primitives, or messing up the boundaries during integration by parts.
4. **Limits:** Failing to resolve indeterminate forms properly, or misapplying L'Hôpital's rule (which is often not allowed in the curriculum; standard limits and factorization must be used).

## Physical Sciences
1. **Units and Conversions:**
   - Using non-SI units in formulas (e.g., leaving volume in mL instead of converting to L or m³ depending on the formula).
   - Confusing minutes/hours with seconds in kinetics.
2. **Graph Reading:** Misinterpreting the asymptote or the initial slope in RC/RL/RLC circuits and chemical kinetics graphs. Not drawing the tangent correctly at t=0.
3. **Sign Conventions:** Mixing up positive and negative signs in thermodynamics, mechanics (work), or when choosing the positive direction in Newton's second law.
4. **Rounding Errors:** Prematurely rounding intermediate values leading to significant deviations in the final result.

## General
- **Time Management:** Spending too much time on a difficult 1-point question and missing easy points in the rest of the exam.
- **Misreading the Prompt:** Missing keywords like "en déduire" (deduce from the previous answer) vs "calculer" (calculate directly), or "justifier" (justify with a theorem or rule).
